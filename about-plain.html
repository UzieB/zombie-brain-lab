Why we made this site

Written by a human

This site is a work in progress. I am a graduate student taking a course on AI-assisted programming. This site is a way for me to explore AI-assisted programming while also, perhaps, creating something useful for students enrolled in a section of Introductory Psychology that I am teaching. One of my concerns with working with an LLM is that it is hard to know what portion of the final product is my contribution and what portion was generated by the predictive text of the LLM. To partially address this concern, I decided to document my process. You can read more about that on How This Was Made.

This is version 1, made without a budget. It used AI for some work that people should do, including drawing the brain scans. See Wrestling with AI [Written by a human:] for more about these problems. [End of human-written text.]

Acknowledgments

This site was inspired by teachers and scientists who used zombies to teach about the brain before us, including:

  • M. M. Masicampo’s post on the Society for the Teaching of Psychology blog, “Zombie Brains and Metacognition,” which led to the idea for this site.
  • Timothy Verstynen and Bradley Voytek’s book Do Zombies Dream of Undead Sheep? A Neuroscientific View of the Zombie Brain, in which two neuroscientists “diagnose” the zombie brain region by region.
  • The University of Nebraska–Lincoln honors seminar “The Neuroscience of Zombies,” in which students identify the nervous-system damage behind zombie behavior in film clips.
  • Caitlin Goodpaster’s article “The Neuroscience of Zombies: Unmasking the Zombie Mind” on Knowing Neurons.
  • The Society for Neuroscience’s free 3D Brain on BrainFacts.org.

The brain science relies mainly on Psychology 2e from OpenStax, a free, openly licensed textbook. Our approach to accessibility draws on the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, the 10 Principles of Disability Justice (Sins Invalid), DisCrit, the Design Justice Network Principles, Building Access (Hamraie), and the Universal Design for Learning Guidelines.

We use a few small Plants vs. Zombies images for teaching, under EA’s content policy for fan projects and fair use. They are © Electronic Arts and are not covered by this site’s Creative Commons license.

Behind the science

Sources don’t always agree. When they disagree, we follow Psychology 2e. Some examples:

  • What’s in the limbic system? Psychology 2e names the hippocampus, amygdala, and hypothalamus as the most important parts; one chapter also includes the thalamus. We teach the first three as the core.
  • Habits. Psychology 2e credits the cerebellum with skill memory. Other research also gives the basal ganglia a big role in habits, so we include both.
  • EEG. Psychology 2e says EEG doesn’t show where activity happens. We say it is poor at showing where, which is more precise.
